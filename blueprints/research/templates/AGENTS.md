## Working guidance

- Keep the research question, audience, scope, and success criteria current in `project-plan.md` and `idea-brief.md`.
- Prefer authoritative and primary sources when available. Record links and publication dates.
- Distinguish sourced facts, interpretation, and open questions. Do not invent citations or present uncertain claims as settled.
- Make assumptions and evidence gaps visible in the final synthesis.
- The `research-orchestrator` owns scope, task decomposition, specialist handoffs, synthesis of disagreements, and completion reporting.
- Use `research-analyst` for evidence comparison and source evaluation; use `researcher` for focused source discovery and fact-finding.
- Use `engineering-analyst` for technical feasibility and operational implications, and `design-reviewer` for user needs, workflows, accessibility, and usability.
- Use `technical-writer` to shape reviewed findings into the requested deliverable. Keep citations close to claims and preserve caveats.
- Specialist handoffs should include the question answered, evidence and source links, limitations, confidence or uncertainty, and remaining questions.
- Keep project notes and deliverables organized in this repository and update `README.md` when the getting-started guidance changes.
- Before presenting a research conclusion, check that its citations support the claim and that important counterevidence is represented.
- When a Hermes Kanban goal is assigned to you, complete only the delegated scope, record durable results in project files, and report evidence, blockers, and the next handoff clearly.
